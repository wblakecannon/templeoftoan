There is a moment in every bass player's life when someone looks at their four-string and says, kindly, "you should get a five."

The Temple wants you to know that this is a sermon about that moment, and that it contains no answer.

## What the fifth string is

The extra string is a low B, tuned below the usual low E. It gives you notes the four-string cannot reach without retuning. For some music, that is the entire point.

## The case for

- You get the low notes without tuning down and rebuilding the feel of the instrument.
- You can stay in one position longer, which some players find more efficient.
- Certain styles lean on those notes heavily, and a good low B is a great sound.

## The case against

- A low B needs a good bass, a good amp, and good technique to be clear instead of mushy. A weak low B string is a very expensive source of mud.
- The neck is wider, the bass is usually heavier, and your hand has more to manage.
- You may never play those notes. The Temple has met many five-strings with a pristine, unplayed B string, which is an altar to intention.

## The real question

Ask what the song needs. If it needs a note you do not have, then you have a reason. If it does not, then the fifth string is a wish, and wishes belong in the same drawer as the pedal you are saving for the right song.

## The sermon

Buy the five if the music calls for it. Buy the four if you are happy. And if someone tells you that you are playing the wrong number of strings, remember that the song is still the only thing that has to sound right.
