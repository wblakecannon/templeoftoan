The Temple does not usually swear. The Temple makes an exception for this.

## What a P-90 is

A P-90 is a single-coil pickup, introduced by Gibson in the mid 1940s, and it is built differently from the single-coil most people picture. It is wider, with a bigger coil, and it usually comes in a flat "soapbar" cover or a "dog ear" version with screw tabs on the sides.

That bigger coil is the whole story. A P-90 tends to sound fuller and louder than a typical Fender-style single-coil, with more midrange, more bite, and a growl that shows up as soon as you lean on it. It is not as thick or compressed as a humbucker. It lives in between, which is exactly why people who care about it will not shut up.

## What it sounds like, in the Temple's opinion

Raw. Honest. A little rude. Clean, it has a chime with some body behind it. Pushed, it gets gritty and loose in a way that makes a simple chord sound like it has something to say. It responds when you pick harder, and it falls apart in a good way when you pick harder still.

It is the sound of a guitar that was not built to be polite.

## The complaints, addressed

- **"It hums."** Yes. It is a single-coil. Some people consider that part of the deal, and there are noise-canceling versions for the ones who do not.
- **"It is not as smooth as a humbucker."** Correct. That was never the assignment.
- **"It is not as sparkly as a Telecaster."** Also correct. It has other virtues.

None of these are defects. They are the personality, and you are free to find the personality unpleasant. You are not free to call it a mistake.

## Who it is for

Anyone who wants a guitar that sounds like a guitar, instead of a perfectly managed product. It tends to be at home in rock, blues, garage, punk, and anywhere someone wants the amp to sound slightly overwhelmed.

## The sermon

If you have not played one, find one and plug it into a loud amp. Play a power chord. Play it again.

If you still do not like P90s after that, the Temple wishes you well, a quiet guitar, and many smooth, reasonable years of sounding fine.
