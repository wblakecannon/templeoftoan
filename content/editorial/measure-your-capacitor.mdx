Before you spend fifty dollars on a capacitor, spend ten on a meter and find out what you already have.

## The claim

A common argument among pedal builders is that much of the audible difference between capacitors comes from how far each part's actual capacitance sits from the number printed on it, rather than from some quality of the capacitor type. A cap labeled 22 nF that is really 25 nF will change a filter. That is real. It is also cheap to fix.

## What you need

A multimeter with a capacitance function, a few parts, and a notepad.

## The procedure

1. Discharge the capacitor, then remove it from the circuit. Measuring in place gives you the circuit, not the part.
2. Set the meter to capacitance and select a range that fits.
3. Touch the leads to the legs and let the reading settle.
4. Write down the reading next to the printed value.
5. Repeat for a few parts of the same type and compare.

## What to expect

Tolerances vary by type. Many film caps are specified to within a few percent and some ceramics are much looser, so expect your readings to wander. The point of the exercise is not to prove a story right. It is to put a number next to it.

## What to do with the result

If your expensive part reads within tolerance and sounds the same as the cheap one, you have saved some money and lost a legend. The Temple considers this a good trade.

If you hear a real difference, measure again. If it survives, you have found something worth writing up, and we would like to read it.
