Every pedalboard is a line. Your guitar goes in one end, and your amp is at the other, and the order of everything in between changes what you hear.

There is a conventional order. It is a starting point, and the people who follow it religiously are usually the ones who have never tried anything else.

## The conventional order

1. **Tuner.** Often first, so it sees the cleanest signal.
2. **Dynamics and filters.** Compressors and wahs, depending on taste.
3. **Gain.** Overdrive, distortion, and fuzz.
4. **Modulation.** Chorus, phaser, flanger, tremolo.
5. **Time-based effects.** Delay, then reverb, near the end.

## Why it works

Gain pedals shape the signal that everything after them has to deal with. Put a delay before a distortion and each echo gets distorted along with the note, which sounds thick and messy. Put the delay after it and the echoes stay clean and trail behind the dirt. Neither is wrong. They are different instruments.

Fuzz pedals are the famous exception. Many fuzzes are sensitive to what comes before them and can sound different, and often worse, behind a buffered pedal. The common advice to put a fuzz first in the chain exists for a reason, and it is one of the few real rules.

## How to find your own order

1. Start with the conventional order.
2. Swap two neighbors and play the same part.
3. Swap them back and play it again.
4. Keep whichever one you wanted to keep playing.

## The sermon

Learn the rule so you understand why you are breaking it. Then break it. The Temple has never met a famous player whose entire sound came from following a flowchart.
