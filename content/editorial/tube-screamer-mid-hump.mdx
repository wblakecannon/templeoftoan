The Tube Screamer is a green overdrive pedal from Ibanez, introduced in the late 1970s, and it has been on a very large number of boards since.

## What it does

It does not simply add distortion. It shapes the sound while it does. A Tube Screamer pushes the midrange and trims some of the lows and highs, which is the famous "mid hump." Run into an already-dirty amp, it tightens the low end and helps the guitar cut through a band instead of vanishing into it.

That is why so many people use it as a booster in front of an amp that is already doing most of the work, rather than as a distortion on its own.

## Why the shame

The Tube Screamer has become a joke in some corners of the gear world, because it is everywhere and because there are a great many clones, modified versions, and "transparent" alternatives. People will tell you that the original is overrated and that a different one is the real answer.

Some of them are right. Most of them are describing small differences in clipping, gain range, and how much of the mid hump they want.

## The relic, as a purchase

The classic circuit is simple enough that nearly every version is a minor variation on the same idea. The fair question is not "which Tube Screamer is the best." It is "do I want this specific EQ curve in front of my amp."

If you do, buy a good one. If you want a sound without a midrange push, you do not need a Tube Screamer, and you do not need to apologize for that.

## Verdict

Learn what the mid hump does, then decide if you want it. The pedal is a tool. The legend is optional.
