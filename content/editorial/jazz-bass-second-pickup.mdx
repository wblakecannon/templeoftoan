The Jazz Bass is Fender's other bass. It arrived after the Precision, with two pickups where the Precision has one, and its reputation has been arguing with the Precision's ever since.

## The one thing to understand

Two pickups means a blend control. Turn it one way and you hear mostly the pickup near the bridge, which is thinner, tighter, and more forward. Turn it the other way and you hear the pickup near the neck, which is rounder and fuller. Somewhere in the middle, with both on, you get a sound that many players treat as the signature of the instrument.

That is the whole cult, really. A single knob that moves the tone between two places, and a congregation that spends years deciding which place to stand in.

## What the argument is really about

Players who love the Jazz Bass tend to talk about its range: you can reach for a punchy, nasal, cutting sound or a warm, round one without changing basses. Players who prefer the Precision tend to talk about its single, thick, undramatic voice, which sits in a mix and stays there.

Both are right. They are describing different jobs.

## The relic, as a purchase

The Jazz Bass family is enormous, from budget models to expensive vintage ones, and the vintage ones carry a tax that is mostly a tax on legend. Before you pay it, borrow one, plug it in, and move the blend knob while someone plays the drums. If you cannot hear the difference between a good modern one and a famous old one in a band setting, the Temple suggests that is information.

## Verdict

If you want options, buy the second pickup. If you want a bass that is already decided, buy the other one. Either way, set the blend where it sounds right in the room, and then leave it alone for a year.
