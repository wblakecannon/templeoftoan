There are two great denominations in the guitar world, and they are divided by a coil of wire.

## The engineering

A **single-coil** pickup uses one coil. It tends to sound bright, clear, and articulate, and it also tends to pick up electrical hum from lights, amps, and nearby electronics.

A **humbucker** uses two coils wired so that hum picked up by one is canceled by the other. That is where the name comes from. The usual result is a quieter pickup with a thicker, stronger sound.

That is the real difference, and it is genuinely real. Everything else is people explaining why they prefer the one they own.

## The two faiths

The single-coil congregation values sparkle and snap. They will accept a little hum as the price of the sound, and some will tell you the hum is part of the character.

The humbucker congregation values weight and quiet. They will accept a thicker tone as the price of the silence, and some will tell you the single-coils sound thin.

Both are describing a trade-off, and both are right about the part they care about.

## The fine print

Pickup design is a field with a lot of variables: magnets, winding, wire, height, and the guitar the pickup is in. A humbucker can sound bright, and a single-coil can sound fat. Hybrids, split coils, and noiseless single-coil designs exist for the people who want to cross the border.

## The sermon

Pick the one that makes you stop thinking about your guitar and start thinking about the song. If you are not sure, borrow one of each and play both at the volume you actually play at, not at the volume you imagine.

The schism is old. The Temple has noticed that the loudest voices on each side tend to own both kinds of guitar.
