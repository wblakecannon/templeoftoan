Toan is tone. It is spelled this way on purpose, by people who know exactly how to spell tone, because the word has become too important to spell normally.

If you have to ask, the short version is this: guitar players believe the sound lives in the object. The pedal, the capacitor, the year of manufacture, the wood the pickguard was cut from. Toan is what you are told you will receive in exchange for your money. The spelling is a small act of ceremony. You do not say "tone" at the altar.

## Where it came from

Nobody can say, and we are not going to pretend. A thread on a bass forum has a poster recalling the spelling turning up around 2007 in a discussion of pickguard material, and asking when tone became toan. The replies are mostly jokes. One suggests that tone is in the fingers and toan is in the expensive useless gear. That is as close to a founding document as the internet has produced.

<PullQuote>
  Tone is in the fingers. Toan is in the expensive useless gear.
</PullQuote>

## Why it stuck

Because it names a real thing. There is the sound you make, and there is the sound you believe you could make if only the right part were inside the right box. The first is tone. The second is toan. The distance between them is where the entire gear industry lives.

The Temple does not ask you to close that distance. We would be out of work.

## What this place is

A publication about the pursuit: the relics, the rigs, the disciples, and the doctrine. We are condescending because the subject has earned it, and affectionate because we have all, at some point, bought the capacitor.

Sacrifice your wallet. Receive toan.
