The Temple has a special pew for bass players. It is at the back, it is very low, and nobody is looking at it.

This is not an insult. It is the doctrine.

## The first truth

When the bass is right, nobody mentions the bass. They say the band sounds big. They say the song feels good. They say the drummer is really locked in. The bass player nods, because the bass player knows exactly who did that.

When the bass is wrong, everybody mentions the bass. Briefly, and usually to the sound engineer.

## The second truth

Low frequencies are physical. Small speakers cannot make them, small rooms smear them, and the tone you worked three weeks to dial in at home will turn into a cloud by the time it reaches the back of a bar. The room is a member of the band, and it did not rehearse.

This is why the gear arguments in the bass world are so intense and so unresolvable. You are trying to buy a sound that is, in large part, decided by a wall.

## The third truth

Guitar players chase toan up the neck. Bass players chase it down, and the ones who find it tend to go very quiet about it.

A good bass sound is usually simple: fresh or deliberately old strings, a bass that stays in tune, a clean path to an amp, and hands that know when not to play. Nearly every expensive upgrade is a bet that the problem is the gear. It is occasionally the gear.

## The sermon

Play to the song. Leave space. Listen to the kick drum like it is your landlord. Then, and only then, go and spend money, because you have earned the right to be wrong about it.

The Temple blesses the low end. We will not be able to hear the blessing, which is the point.
