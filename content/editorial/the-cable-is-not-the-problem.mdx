Every forum has a thread about cables. It is the longest thread, it has no ending, and every participant is certain.

The Temple has read it. We will save you the afternoon.

## What is actually true

A passive guitar pickup is a fragile source. It has a high output impedance, which means it does not drive a load strongly, and the cable you plug into it becomes part of the circuit. A cable has capacitance. A longer cable has more of it. More capacitance, with a passive pickup, tends to roll off the top end.

So a very long, cheap cable can make a guitar sound duller. That is physics, it is measurable, and it is the reason a pedal with a buffer in front of a long run can change the sound in a way people notice.

## What is also true

A cable that is not broken and not absurdly long is mostly doing its job. The difference between two sensible cables is, for most players in most rooms, smaller than the difference between two nights of sleep.

The expensive cable is not a lie. It is usually better built, quieter, and survives being stepped on. Those are real virtues. "Tone" is not always one of them.

## What is not true

That a cable has been "broken in." That a directional arrow on a cable changes the sound of your guitar. That the specific brand your idol used is the reason their records sound like that.

## The sermon

Buy one good cable. Keep it short. Coil it properly. When something sounds wrong, check the cable first, because it is the cheapest thing in your rig to replace and the most likely to be broken.

Then stop reading the thread.
