There is a clinical-sounding name for what you are about to do. Gear acquisition syndrome, or GAS, is commonly credited to Walter Becker of Steely Dan, who used it in a 1996 Guitar Player article. It is not a diagnosis. It is a joke that turned out to be a map.

## The symptoms

You have a pedal you use constantly. You have a pedal you use once a year. You have a pedal you are saving for the right song, and the song has not been written, and may never be.

You have watched a stranger demonstrate a pedal for fourteen minutes and concluded that the stranger's room has good acoustics, and that this is what you were missing.

## The research, such as it is

Academics have looked at this. Researchers have surveyed electric guitar players and written about the practice as a set of habits around identity, desire, community, and craft, closer to culture than disorder. That is a generous framing and we accept it, because it means the congregation is not sick. It is merely devout.

## The sermon

You do not need another pedal. You may want one, and wanting is allowed. The Temple does not forbid desire. We only ask that you notice the exact moment the wanting arrives, usually at 11:40 at night, usually after a video, and that you let it sit for one week.

Most of the time it will leave. When it does not, it was a real want, and you may proceed with our blessing and no further comment.
